# Learnings — 2026-09-03

## Incident investigation: "Celery queues overloaded" (prod worker-med CrashLoopBackOff)

Process lessons from a long, self-correcting investigation (user pushed back correctly 3×):

1. **Baseline a log's historical rate before calling it root cause.** I anchored on a scary Manager
   warning (`Team [1] does not belong to cluster assigned to principal …`) and even built a
   "9 deployments affected" story on it. It was **chronic noise** — flat at ~1,600/min for 7 days,
   *lower* during the incident than the day before. One `/api/v2/logs/analytics/aggregate` timeseries
   would have killed the hypothesis immediately. Correlation-in-the-moment ≠ causation.

2. **`phase: Running` / `list_resources(pod)` hides CrashLoopBackOff.** My first-turn claim "all 10 pods
   Running, no crashes" was wrong — pods are nominally Running *between* restarts. Use
   `get_resource(pod)` → `container_statuses[].state_reason` / `restart_count`, or k8s `BackOff` events,
   or the `kubernetes.containers.restarts` metric.

3. **Verify blast radius from an INDEPENDENT signal, not the first one you found.** The auth-noise logs
   "showed" 9 deployments; the real blast radius was **4** deployments, found via
   `sum:kubernetes.containers.restarts{kube_deployment:*-superset-worker-med} by {kube_namespace}`.

4. **`list_preset_deployments` (bulk) returns `workspace_count: 0` and `app_versions: null` for every
   deployment — unreliable.** Use `get_deployment(deployment_id)` for authoritative per-deployment
   `workspace_count` (078d77ab was 231, not 0) and `app_versions` (SHA/version + changed_on).

5. **Prove code claims by diffing at the exact deployed SHA** (per skills/postmortem-from-p0-p1.md).
   Found the `CELERY_CONFIG` warnings in `preset-io/superset-private:superset/initialization/__init__.py`
   at `sha_superset=76151bea`. Also checked the `sys.exit(1)` "Refusing to start" guards — then
   **disproved** them via a log search (no "Refusing to start" line). Check, don't assert.

6. **Know the vantage point's limits.** Outside-cluster telemetry (DD logs/metrics + Birds MCP) could
   prove crashloop/blast-radius/trigger/timing, but NOT the exact fatal instruction — the worker dies
   after a full boot with no traceback/OOM/probe-kill. That needs `kubectl … -o json`
   `lastState.terminated.exitCode` from inside the cluster. Say so; don't paper over it.

7. **Confirmed root cause = worker-med OOMKilled at its 3Gi limit** (KSM
   `kubernetes_state.container.status_report.count.terminated{reason:oomkilled}` = recurring, 147 pts/3h).
   A 6.1.0.7 base-memory increase (suspect: MCP native-catalog / protocol dep stack) pushes worker-med
   (12 prefork, tightest 3Gi limit) over the edge. **Version-driven, not size-driven** — a 5-workspace
   6.1.0.7 deployment crashes while a 255-workspace 6.0.0.31 one is healthy. Fix: roll to 6.1.0.6 OR
   raise worker-med mem limit / lower concurrency.

8. **Don't trust a `max ... by {group}` aggregate as a discriminator.** RSS/working_set `by {version}`
   showed ~3.2GB for 6.1.0.5/.6/.7 alike, which I misread as "no version difference → rollback won't
   help." Wrong: the per-version *max* just meant *some* pod on each version neared the limit; only .7
   pods actually OOMKill (the `reason:oomkilled` terminated metric was the real discriminator). The
   clean test was per-deployment correlation (crash vs workspace_count vs version), not a group-max.

9. **I flip-flopped twice on OOM** ("not OOM" → unproven → "OOM but size-driven" → "OOM, version-driven").
   Each flip came from a NEW measurement, which is fine — but the lesson is to reach for the *direct*
   signal early (container terminated-reason via KSM; per-deployment crash-vs-attribute correlation)
   instead of inferring from medians/aggregates.

10. **CORRECTION: it was NOT OOM.** I concluded "OOMKilled at 3Gi" from (a) a `by-version max`
    working_set aggregate that swept in healthy fleet pods and (b) a KSM `reason:oomkilled` gauge that
    turned out stale (flat at 9 while restarts climbed +8). A peer investigator's direct per-pod
    working_set reading (~470 MB) contradicted it; my own per-pod re-check confirmed crashing pods peak
    ≤1.5 GB under the 3Gi limit. **Real root cause: a fatal native crash in the 6.1.0.7 #4914 MCP
    dependency stack (mcp 1.29.1 / fastmcp 3.4.7 / anyio) loaded at worker boot via
    initialize_core_mcp_dependencies(), fatal in the 12-way prefork worker-med** (silent, post-Ready,
    intermittent, prefork-only). Lesson: a peer's DIRECT measurement beat my metric-inferred conclusion
    — per-pod > by-version max; direct working_set > a KSM reason gauge; and OOM-on-boot is
    incompatible with logs that reach celery `ready` first. #4925 sizing is defense-in-depth, not the fix.
